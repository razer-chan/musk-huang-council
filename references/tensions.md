# Disagreements, changes of mind, costs, and what not to copy

Codes refer to `index.md`. Paragraphs marked "compiler's judgement" are not things either man said. They are the view of the person who compiled the index.

## 1. Where the two disagree

### Will AI leave people without work?

- **Jensen's** main line: a job's tasks and its purpose are different things, and it is the tasks that get automated (AI-01, AI-03). You are more likely to lose out to someone who uses AI well than to AI itself (AI-02). Every job will change (AI-09).
- He has also conceded ground. A net gain in jobs does not protect any particular person (AI-04). If humanity had nothing new it wanted to do, automation would indeed put more people out of work (AI-05).
- **Musk's** main line: work will eventually become optional and money will stop mattering (AI-11, AI-14, AI-15). Work that does not involve moving physical things goes first (AI-13). He was saying this in 2019 (AI-16).
- Asked where meaning would come from in that world, Musk says he does not know (AI-12), and offers growing vegetables as an analogy (ME-04).
- **Where they agree**: both urge people to learn to use AI now (LE-15, ED-13), and both value the ability to ask good questions and broad knowledge (AI-07, AI-17, ED-09).
- **Compiler's judgement**: Jensen sells AI computing and Musk sells robots and cars. Each man's predictions favour his own business. Say so when answering questions of this kind.

### Is college worth it?

- **Musk** does not treat a degree as evidence of ability (ED-04, PE-03) and has said college is overvalued (ED-05, ED-20).
- He also recommends studying engineering and physics (ED-10, LE-04) and wants critical thinking taught early (ED-17, ED-18, ED-19). What he doubts is the credential, not the learning.
- **Jensen** says that on the statistics, finishing school is still the better route (ED-11), and tells high-school students to do well in their classes (ED-14).
- ED-05 was said at a political town hall, a different setting from the others.

### Should you start a company?

- **Musk**: if you need encouragement, do not (RI-02, RI-03). Getting a job is far easier (RI-04). The experience is brutal (RE-08). For a first company, pick something that needs little capital (RI-06).
- **Jensen**: he has twice said he would not start again (CO-10, CO-11). He also counts ignorance as an entrepreneur's advantage (RI-09), and says that at the time he did not feel he was putting his family at risk (RI-10).
- **Where they agree**: neither treats making money as the reason to do it (Jensen: MO-06; Musk: US-04). Both put usefulness first (US-02, US-03, US-08).

### Is suffering good for you?

- **Jensen** treats suffering and low expectations as advantages (RE-01, RE-02, RE-07), yet also says to leave the pain behind (RE-05) and not to rehearse setbacks in advance (RE-06).
- **Musk** does not romanticise failure (RE-12) and says you should give up when you are doing the wrong thing (RE-09).

### Is there enough time?

- **Jensen** talks about having plenty of time (ME-08, FO-05) and also says he is always anxious and always tired (CO-07, CO-08). The tension is within the same person. Give both sides.

## 2. The costs they have described themselves

**Musk**

- His workload is not right for everyone, and he would like to work somewhat less (CO-02).
- With no time off, he makes worse decisions (CO-03).
- The pain of long hours rises exponentially (CO-06).
- The nearest he came to a nervous breakdown (CO-05).
- Looking back, he would tell his younger self to pause now and then (CO-04).
- Running two companies at once is a bad idea (FO-07).

**Jensen**

- Permanently anxious (CO-07) and tired all the time (CO-08).
- He missed nearly all of his children's tournaments and practices (CO-09).
- Being CEO is a lifetime of sacrifice (CO-12).
- He would not start the company again (CO-10, CO-11).

When the topic is work intensity, persistence or starting a company, include at least one or two of these.

## 3. Views they have changed

- **Jensen on whether everyone should learn to program**: he came to say nearly the opposite of the old advice, that computers should understand human language instead (ED-12). His advice became: learn to work with AI first (LE-15).
- **Musk on self-driving timelines**: in 2019 he said it would be done that year and that he was certain (PR-01). In 2024 he admitted he had been far too optimistic (PR-02).
- **Musk on work intensity**: he has spoken of working every waking hour and written that world-changing work does not happen on a forty-hour week (CO-01, CO-13). Later he said this is not for everyone (CO-02), and that he would tell his younger self to pause occasionally (CO-04).
- **Musk on politics**: he admits he stayed too long and got carried away (FO-08).

When a user cites something from their earlier years, check here for a later correction.

## 4. What not to copy

This section is the compiler's judgement.

- **Eighty to a hundred hours a week.** Both have described the cost (see section 2).
- **Putting all your savings in.** Musk has described surviving on the last of the money (RE-10, RE-11), but by then he had the proceeds from selling an earlier company and something to fall back on. Most people do not.
- **No one-on-ones and dozens of direct reports.** This is Jensen's organisational design for one particular company (PE-05), not something an ordinary manager can lift.
- **Wishing suffering on people.** In the same passage as RE-01, where Jensen says he hopes students get to suffer, he also says he does not know how to teach resilience. It is an observation, not a method.
- **Making financial decisions on their predictions.** See section 5.

Two more things to keep in mind:

- **Survivorship bias.** People who used the same methods and failed do not leave hundreds of hours of interviews.
- **Jensen himself is reluctant to advise.** He says other people's circumstances differ too much from his (RI-11).

## 5. How to use their predictions

- Musk has said that some of his timelines are aspirations rather than predictions (PR-03), and that people are welcome to replay his words later and see whether he was wrong (PR-04).
- Jensen says he is poor at forecasting and better at estimating the size of an opportunity (PR-05).
- **Compiler's judgement**: read their statements as "the direction is probably this", not as "this will happen within N years". When citing any prediction with a date attached, give the pair PR-01 and PR-02 alongside it.
