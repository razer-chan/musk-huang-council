# Ten principles that run through both

Use this when the user wants general advice. Grouping the material into ten is the compiler's work; the evidence under each comes from `index.md`.

Whenever you present a principle, include its "do not copy" line.

## 1. Put usefulness first, ahead of being impressive or well known

- Musk describes the goal of a career as optimising for usefulness, measured as how much you help multiplied by how many people you help (US-01, US-02, US-03).
- Jensen describes his company's purpose as helping others do their life's work, and a leader's job as helping others succeed (US-08, US-10).
- Do not copy: useful does not mean pleasing. Musk himself says being useful is hard (US-11).

## 2. Use first principles for big decisions; analogy is fine for small ones

- Break the problem down to the facts you are most sure of, and reason up from there (TH-01, TH-03). Jensen falls back on the same thing when things are uncertain (TH-08, TH-12).
- Do not copy: Musk says it takes real effort and cannot be done for everything (TH-02). Jensen says some problems are too complex for it (TH-13).

## 3. Assume you are partly wrong, and build a system for finding out

- Musk: he takes it that he is always somewhat wrong (FB-01), asks for negative feedback (FB-02), and warns that ego cuts the feedback loop (FB-03).
- Jensen: intellectual honesty means being able to admit a mistake (FB-09). Admitting one and asking for help saved his company (FB-10). You cannot change course without admitting error (FB-12).
- Do not copy: listening to feedback is not the same as obeying all of it. Jensen also insists on holding a core belief and checking it daily (FB-15).

## 4. Choose a craft and work at it for a decade

- Jensen hopes people find a craft they want to spend a lifetime perfecting, with no hurry to decide (ME-07). The Kyoto gardener story (ME-08). He admires people who do ordinary work with great craft (ME-10).
- Musk's version is to look for where your talents and interests overlap (LE-03).
- Do not copy: this is not advice to lock in a direction while very young.

## 5. Do the most important thing first each day; be willing to give things up

- Jensen does his highest-priority work first each morning (FO-01). Deciding what to give up is at the core of success (FO-02, FO-03).
- Musk talks about signal over noise (FO-06), and admits he stayed too long in something that was not his main work (FO-08).
- Do not copy: giving things up only works once you know what your main line is.

## 6. Lower your expectations and raise your tolerance, but do not make suffering the goal

- Jensen: low expectations are an advantage, and character comes from having suffered (RE-01, RE-02). Learn the lesson, then leave the pain behind (RE-05).
- Musk: persistence is not unconditional; give up when you are doing the wrong thing (RE-09).
- Do not copy: both have spelled out the cost (CO-02, CO-07). Tolerance is not the same as running yourself into the ground.

## 7. Use AI as a daily tutor; separate a job's tasks from its purpose

- Jensen: what gets automated is the task, not the purpose (AI-01). You are more likely to lose out to someone who uses AI well (AI-02). He learns with AI every day (LE-11, LE-12) and insists it still be checked (AI-10).
- Musk: broad knowledge is what tells you which questions to put to the machine (ED-09).
- Do not copy: the two disagree about whether work will disappear. See section 1 of `tensions.md`.

## 8. Learn fundamentals, read widely, stay a student

- Musk: read a lot and talk to people (LE-01, LE-02). Apply the thinking tools of physics everywhere (LE-04).
- Jensen: he casts himself as a good student (LE-08). Whatever he does not understand is probably one textbook away (LE-09).
- Do not copy: reading widely is for finding a direction, not for appearing learned.

## 9. Build something that works, then delete, then optimise, and automate last

- Musk: build a prototype you can demonstrate (BU-01). Question the requirements first, then delete (BU-02, BU-03). He admits he has often done the steps in the wrong order (BU-04).
- Jensen: unless it exists, nobody can show up to use it (BU-09).
- Do not copy: the sequence was worked out for engineering and manufacturing. In other fields, what can be deleted depends on the real constraints.

## 10. Take affordable risks while your obligations are few; discount everyone's timelines

- Musk: take risks before you have family obligations (RI-01). Better to be an optimist and wrong (ME-01).
- Jensen: choose something hard enough, suited to you, and loved despite the pain (RI-07). Where there are no customers there are no competitors (RI-08).
- Do not copy: their own timing predictions have often been too optimistic (PR-01, PR-02, PR-05). "Affordable" means a failure that does not damage your health or your basic living.
